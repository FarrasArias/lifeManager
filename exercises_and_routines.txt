╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

  1. Cardio
     Sets: 3 | Reps: 10
     Description: A practical 20-minute protocol on your recumbent bike: Start with 3–4 minutes of easy pedaling to warm up. Then do intervals: pedal hard (high resistance, around 85–90% effort) for 30–60 seconds, followed by easy pedaling (low resistance, around 40–50% effort) for 1.5–2 minutes. Repeat 5–8 rounds. Finish with 2–3 minutes of easy cooldown pedaling. The protocol you described (90% for a minute, 40% for two minutes) is basically perfect — that's very close to what the research uses. One important caveat though: since you're in rehab for spinal compression, please run the HIIT idea by your physio or rehab specialist before going all-in. Rehab professionals typically recommend starting with 10–15 minutes of low resistance, then building up from there. (Vanswe Fitness) The recumbent bike itself is spine-safe, but spiking your effort to 90% could involve bracing or tensing through your core in ways your spine might not be ready for yet. Start with milder intervals (say 70% effort instead of 90%) and build up over a few weeks as your physio clears you.
