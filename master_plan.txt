║  SECTION 1 — TRAINING PHILOSOPHY SHIFT                                     ║
║  Why you should move to moderate loads + higher reps                        ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

  YOU SAID: "I'm reaching the point where I've been increasing weight to
  a point where I'm close to getting hurt instead of close to failure."

  THE RESEARCH SAYS YOU'RE RIGHT TO BE CONCERNED:

  1. MUSCLE GROWTH IS REP-RANGE INDEPENDENT (when effort is equal)
     A 2024 meta-analysis of 47 studies found NO significant difference
     in muscle hypertrophy between low-load (15-30 reps) and high-load
     (6-12 reps) when sets are taken close to failure. What matters is
     EFFORT, not load.

  2. MODERATE REPS (8-15) ARE THE SWEET SPOT FOR YOUR SITUATION
     • Heavy enough to build real strength
     • Light enough that joints, discs, and nerves aren't crushed
     • Easier to push near failure safely (form doesn't collapse)
     • Lower injury risk with spinal compression issues

  3. REPS IN RESERVE (RIR) — YOUR NEW INTENSITY TOOL
     Instead of chasing max weight, use RIR:
     ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
     │  Compound lifts (cables/machines): Stop at 2-3 RIR      │
     │  Secondary compounds:              Stop at 1-2 RIR      │
     │  Isolation exercises:               Push to 0-1 RIR      │
     │  NEVER train to absolute failure on spinal-loading moves │
     └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  4. WHY THIS IS SAFER FOR YOUR SPINE
     Research shows spinal position deteriorates rapidly at failure on
     compound movements. With disc bulges at L3-L5 and neuroforaminal
     narrowing, even slight form breakdown under heavy load risks
     worsening nerve root contact. Moderate loads with controlled form
     let you push muscles hard while keeping your spine safe.

  5. PROGRESSION RULE (UPDATED)
     Old: Add weight when you hit max reps
     New: Add reps first (8→10→12→15), THEN add small weight
          increment (2-5kg) and drop back to 8 reps. Slower but
          safer and equally effective for muscle growth.

  6. MACHINES AND CABLES OVER FREE WEIGHTS (where possible)
     Machines stabilize your spine, isolate target muscles, and remove
     the spinal compression risk of positioning free weights. Your gym
     already uses mostly cables — this is perfect for your situation.
     The research confirms: machines allow you to strengthen arms/legs
     without compressing your back.

  WHY RECUMBENT BIKE: Research confirms it's the single best cardio
  option for spinal stenosis. The reclined seat and back support
  minimize spinal compression. A 2018 study found 58.3% of stenosis
  patients showed clinical improvement after 3 months of cycling.

  SCHEDULE: 3× per week — Monday (post-Push), Wednesday (post-Pull),
  Friday (recovery day). Always after upper body work, never after
  gym legs day.

  │  • 10-14 min steady state at low-moderate resistance (RPE 4-5)      │
  │  • 2 min cool-down, easy pedal                                      │
  │  • Heart rate target: 50-60% max HR (~94-112 bpm at age 33)        │

║  SECTION 7 — CRITICAL SAFETY RULES                                         ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

  1. PAIN RULE (unchanged, still essential):
     If ANY exercise causes pain above 3/10 during or 24 hours after,
     drop the weight 20% and hold for 2 weeks before trying to progress.

  2. LEFT ARM RULE (new):
     If you feel coldness, tingling, or increased pain in your left arm
     during any exercise, STOP that exercise immediately. Switch to
     single-arm (right only) or skip it. Log which exercises trigger it.

  3. SPINE RULE (new):
     Never let your lower back round under load. If you can't maintain
     neutral spine, the weight is too heavy — no exceptions. This
     applies especially to leg press, RDL, and cable pull-through.

  4. NERVE FLOSSING RULE:
     Nerve glides should feel like a MILD stretch, never sharp pain.
     These are oscillations, not static stretches — never hold at end
     range. If symptoms increase during flossing, reduce range of motion.

  5. FATIGUE MANAGEMENT:
     You're investing 2 hours/day which is significant. If you feel
     overall fatigue accumulating (poor sleep, increased pain, low
     motivation for >3 days), take a deload week: halve all gym weights,
     keep physio the same, increase rest.

  6. PARASPINAL MUSCLE PRIORITY:
     Your MRI showed thinned paraspinal + psoas muscles with fatty
     infiltration. Rebuilding these is arguably the #1 priority for
     long-term spine health. Every core exercise in your physio program
     targets these. Don't skip core work — it's not optional, it's
     treatment.
